# Parlume meeting preview

- **Audience**: Project administrators and operators
- **Owner**: Engineering team

Parlume is a default-off, project-scoped way to invite the built-in Fabric Agent or a project-bound custom agent to a Microsoft Teams meeting by link. Each project has a **Parlume page** at `/projects/{id}/parlume`, linked from Meeting Digest: administrators invite and remove the bot there, and readers review sessions, requests, and spend. While Parlume is joining, the page shows the invite's progress (invite sent, waiting in the lobby), then a live card with the elapsed time, agent, mode, spend so far and the session's latest requests. Below it, a Sessions and Requests panel lists sessions five at a time and filters requests by session and by question or action. The bot appears as **Fabric Parlume**, records and transcribes meeting audio, responds to “Hey Fabric,” “Hey Parlume,” or “Hey Fabric Parlume,” (also when transcription writes the opening “Hey” as “A”, “Eh” or “Uh”, as it did for “A Parlume: how many open features…” on staging) and saves a transcript and generated notes to the project. The join URL is sent to the meeting provider but is not stored in Fabric.

## Organization setup (Settings → Meeting assistant)

An organization configures its own meeting bot and voice at `/app/{organization}/settings/meeting-assistant`. The page exists only while `PARLUME_MEETINGS` is on for the organization. Members can read it; only owners and admins can change it or run its tests. Settings saved here take precedence over the operator's environment defaults, as described for each item below.

- **Meeting bot.** The organization's Attendee API key (stored encrypted, shown only as its last four characters), an optional Attendee URL for a self-hosted Attendee (https only; empty is `https://app.attendee.dev`), and the Teams sign-in mode: anonymous guest, sign in only when the meeting requires it, or always sign in. The URL and sign-in mode belong to the organization's own key and apply only with it: without one, invitations use the operator's Attendee project as a whole, and the operator's key is never sent to an organization's URL. Removing the key removes all three. The saved key is only ever sent to the URL it was saved with: changing the URL, including back to the default, needs the key typed again, and **Test connection** tries a different URL only with a key typed alongside it. **Test connection** calls Attendee's read-only bot list (`GET /api/v1/bots`), creates no bot and costs nothing, and reports a rejected key apart from an unreachable instance. A URL that points at a private or internal address is refused unless the operator lists its host in `PARLUME_ATTENDEE_ALLOWED_HOSTS`.
- **Voice.** The name of the Azure AI Foundry deployment that serves the realtime voice model (lowercase letters, digits, dots and hyphens; empty means a deployment named after the model, `gpt-realtime-2.1`). The Azure AI Foundry and OpenAI keys themselves are set in **Settings → AI providers**. **Test realtime voice** mints a short-lived realtime client secret on each configured host, the way a meeting does, but opens no session and bills nothing, and shows each host's own refusal. Usage and pricing always record the model id, whatever the deployment is called.

Two things can only be done at the vendor, and Fabric cannot do them for you:

1. **The Teams sign-in account.** The Microsoft 365 account the bot signs in with is entered as the Teams bot login in the Attendee dashboard; Attendee has no API for it. Choose a sign-in mode in Fabric only once that account is set up there.
2. **The Azure realtime deployment.** The deployment must exist in the organization's Azure AI Foundry resource. Without it Azure refuses the mint, and Parlume falls through to the organization's OpenAI key or the classic voice.

The Parlume page lists whatever an invitation is still missing (the meeting bot, a voice provider, or the operator's media bridge), each with a link to the settings page that fixes it and back. A member who cannot change settings is told that an owner or admin can.

## Auto-join a linked meeting series

Parlume can join every occurrence of a recurring Teams meeting on its own, so nobody has to paste the link each time. It is set per linked series; there is no switch that joins every meeting.

**Turning it on.** In the project's **Meeting Digest** tab, choose **Configure meetings**. Each linked series has a **Parlume joins automatically** switch, shown while both `PARLUME_MEETINGS` and `MEETING_AGENDA` are on for the organization. Switching it on reveals the same two choices as the invite card: **Read-only** or **Actions**, and **Chat suggestions**. Changing either saves straight away. The switch needs the same permission as inviting Parlume by hand (project administration). It is refused, with the reason and where to fix it, when you have no Microsoft connection (Settings → Integrations) or when Parlume is not ready to join meetings (a meeting bot in Settings → Meeting assistant, a voice provider in Settings → AI Providers, and the operator's media bridge). It cannot be switched on for a series whose syncing is stopped, because the scheduler skips those; resume syncing in the project's Settings → Meeting Transcript Sync first. A switch already on when syncing stops stays on but does nothing until syncing resumes, and the series says so.

**Whose calendar and permissions.** The person who turns the switch on becomes the series' enabler, and the panel names them. Fabric reads their Teams calendar, through their Microsoft connection in the project's organization, to find each occurrence, and every join runs as them: their project permissions, their voice provider and the built-in **Fabric Agent — this project**. The series must therefore be on the enabler's own calendar; an occurrence they were not invited to is not found and not joined. Anyone who changes the mode or suggestions later becomes the enabler instead. If the enabler loses project administration, joins stop and the series says so until someone turns the switch on again; if their account is deleted, the series asks to be turned off and on again.

**Timing.** A Temporal schedule (`parlume-auto-join-planner`) runs every five minutes. It reads each enabler's calendar once for the window from ten minutes ago to fifteen minutes ahead and matches occurrences to the linked series by join link. For each occurrence that is not cancelled it schedules one join about a minute before the start, or immediately if that has passed. It still joins up to ten minutes after the start, and leaves alone an occurrence that began longer ago. Each occurrence gets at most one session, however many planner runs see it. Just before joining, Fabric reads the enabler's calendar again for two minutes either side of the scheduled start, and joins only if the occurrence is still there, not cancelled and starting within a minute of that time. A cancelled occurrence is skipped; a moved one is skipped at its old time and planned again at its new one. Fabric then checks everything else again: the switch is still on and the series still syncing, both flags are on, the project is not in Read-only mode, the enabler still has permission, Parlume is ready, and the occurrence has no session yet.

**One Parlume per meeting.** Inviting Parlume by hand with the link of a linked series records that series on the session. While any session of a series is in its meeting or on its way (pending, joining, active or leaving), auto-join does not send a second bot, and a second invitation is refused with "Parlume is already in this meeting." Sessions do not store their meeting link, so this check covers linked series only: two hand invitations to a meeting that is not a linked series are not detected.

**Read-only mode.** A bot joining a meeting counts as a write to a connected source, so auto-join does not join while the project is in Read-only mode, and the series says so. Calendar reads continue. Inviting Parlume by hand is not affected.

**The lobby.** An automatic join behaves like an invite: the organizer may have to admit Parlume from the lobby, and Parlume leaves after ten minutes unadmitted, ending the session as **Not admitted from the lobby**. Auto-join records every occurrence without anyone inviting Parlume again, so tell the series' regular attendees before you turn it on.

**Seeing what happened.** Sessions an auto-join started appear on the Parlume page's sessions list marked **Auto-joined · {series}**. When a join could not happen, the series in Configure meetings shows why, with a link to the settings page that fixes it: a Microsoft connection that needs reconnecting, a calendar that could not be read (retried every five minutes, or, just before a join, three times ten seconds apart before that occurrence is skipped), Read-only mode, Parlume not being ready, the enabler's lost permission, or the meeting provider's own reason for refusing the bot. A cancelled or moved occurrence, or a meeting Parlume is already in, is skipped without a notice. The notice clears on the next successful join, when the switch is set again, or, for a calendar problem, as soon as the calendar can be read. The web API and worker log every decision as `parlume.auto_join.*` events.

## Operator setup

1. Deploy the database migration and the updated web API, Temporal worker, and Cloudflare PartyServer/Durable Object together. The Durable Object is event-driven; no dedicated media process runs while there is no meeting. The PartyServer Worker needs its Workers AI binding (`AI`, declared in `party-cf/wrangler.toml`), which it uses to transcribe meetings.
2. The media bridge, the webhook and the service secret are operator settings only. The Attendee settings are environment fallbacks for organizations that have not saved their own: create an API key for the hosted [Attendee](https://app.attendee.dev) project that will run the bots, and configure it as `PARLUME_ATTENDEE_API_KEY` in the web API; `PARLUME_ATTENDEE_BASE_URL` optionally points at a self-hosted Attendee, and `PARLUME_ATTENDEE_ALLOWED_HOSTS` lists any private host an organization's Attendee URL may use. Fabric derives the public bridge URL from `NEXT_PUBLIC_PARTYKIT_HOST` and the callback URL from `APP_URL` (or `NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL`). `PARLUME_BRIDGE_WS_URL` and `PARLUME_CALLBACK_URL` remain optional explicit overrides. The existing `AGENT_SERVICE_SECRET` must match between web, Temporal, and PartyServer, and PartyServer needs `FABRIC_API_URL` for the internal callback routes.
3. Ensure the Temporal worker's `NEXT_PUBLIC_PARTYKIT_HOST` points at the same PartyServer host. Parlume speaks with **Deepgram Aura-2** (voice `orpheus`, `@cf/deepgram/aura-2-en`), synthesized inside the bridge on Workers AI, which needs no organization credential. If Aura cannot speak a sentence, the rest of the reply goes through the organization's **Vercel AI Gateway** provider (`openai/tts-1`, the same credential and billing as its language models) and then its **OpenAI Direct** key. The invite fails before starting a provider bot if no Attendee key resolves (the organization's or the environment's) or the organization has no Azure AI Foundry, OpenAI or Vercel AI Gateway provider to voice it. When the provider refuses the bot, the invite shows Attendee's own reason. A reply that cannot be spoken is sent to the meeting chat instead, and the request records why and whether the chat showed it.
4. Enable `PARLUME_MEETINGS` for the test organization. It defaults off. The default choice is **Fabric Agent — this project**, which uses the project's current knowledge and Fabric Agent behavior. Custom agents remain available when owned by the inviter and bound to the project through `project-context`.
5. Paste an ordinary Teams meeting link. A `teams.microsoft.com/meet/…` link must keep its `?p=` passcode, which Attendee needs to join; `/l/meetup-join/…` links work as copied. By default the bot joins as an anonymous external guest. The organizer may have to admit it from the lobby, and it leaves on its own after ten minutes unadmitted; the session then ends as **Not admitted from the lobby** with "Nobody admitted Parlume from the lobby within 10 minutes." Other join failures Attendee reports (`could_not_join_meeting` with a sub type such as `request_to_join_denied`, `meeting_not_found`, `login_required` or `bot_login_attempt_failed`) get their own plain message; an unknown one names its sub type. An anonymous guest cannot join a meeting that requires signed-in or tenant-only guests, and it has no chat box in a channel meeting or in a meeting whose chat policy excludes anonymous users.
6. Optional, for those meetings: run the bot signed in. Create a Microsoft 365 account for the bot, named **Fabric Parlume** so its posts and voice are recognized as the bot's own; for channel meetings, add that account to the team. Enter the account's credentials as the Teams bot login on the Attendee project, then choose the sign-in mode in **Settings → Meeting assistant** (with the organization's own Attendee key), or, for the operator's Attendee project, set `PARLUME_ATTENDEE_TEAMS_LOGIN` in the web API to `always` (sign in for every meeting) or `only_if_required` (sign in only when the meeting refuses an anonymous guest). Fabric then creates each bot with `teams_settings: { use_login: true, login_mode }`. Unset keeps the anonymous guest. Any other value makes the invite fail before a bot is created; the inviter is told the meeting bot settings could not be read, and the web API log (`parlume.start.attendee_settings_invalid`) names the setting.

For a smoke test, use a disposable meeting with consenting participants. Confirm the bot joins as **Fabric Parlume**, captures final segments, answers each of the three wake phrases aloud, stops on request, and leaves a project transcript and notes. Check that a flagged-off organization cannot invoke the API. Ask a question that spans a natural pause, for example “Hey Parlume, what is the name … of the project?”, and confirm one answer arrives after the sentence ends. Joining, the agent's answers and speech synthesis were verified on staging on 2026-10-02 with the previous meeting provider. Audible playback and speaker attribution through Attendee are pending verification on staging.

## Boundaries

- The invite form tells the inviter that Parlume records and transcribes the meeting, and that participants should be told before it is invited. It is a notice, not a gate: there is nothing to tick, and Fabric stores no consent record.
- The inviter must have project administration permission. Any attendee able to speak in the meeting can request a spoken answer from the selected project's knowledge; the bot cannot verify each attendee's Fabric identity.
- Parlume is a voice adapter around the selected agent's normal execution path. Each request reloads the current active custom-agent version or the built-in agent's preferences. Configured instructions, knowledge, tools, models and permission checks remain owned by that agent. Agent changes apply to subsequent requests.
- Invitations default to **Read-only**, which refuses every change. Choosing **Actions** lets attendees ask for changes, and Parlume makes each one as soon as it is asked for, using the inviter's current project permissions. There is no confirmation step: the change runs when the agent calls the tool, so anyone able to speak in the meeting can cause a change the inviter could make, and an instruction that reaches the agent through the transcript or a tool result could as well. A change that names another project is refused. A provider speaker label is continuity information, not a verified Fabric identity; people sharing a microphone may share that label, and a change asked for by a speaker Teams gave no label for is recorded as unattributed.
- Each change is recorded before it runs as a `ParlumeAction` with the requester's label, the tool, its arguments, and later its outcome, and writes an audit entry (`project.parlume.action_confirmed`, shown as “Parlume action executed”; the key keeps its original name). An identical call in the same turn runs once. A result that names what it made (for example a work item's identifier and link) and carries no error is recorded as completed; an uncertain outcome such as a timeout is recorded without automatic retry. Check the destination before requesting an uncertain action again. An interruption before dispatch makes no change.
- Creating a work item answers within a few seconds. The item is created from what was said, as the agent named its kind, and its body is drafted afterwards by the same workflow that rewrites a body after a type conversion; the prior body is kept as a version, and the draft is discarded if someone edited the item meanwhile. The agent says what it created and, when asked for the link, sends it to the meeting chat.
- The Fabric Agent in a meeting also has 38 of the Fabric portal's tools: who the inviter is and their organization, the projects, features and their tasks and status, bugs, documents, project contexts, coding instructions (read, propose a change, add a lesson), project knowledge search, workspaces, the inviter's AI chats and their connected servers. They come through the Fabric MCP gateway on a grant the web app signs for each turn. The grant names the meeting's project and the scopes those tools need; the gateway keeps only the scopes within its own ceiling and admits the grant only when the inviter can open that project in the organization acted in. Every call is then held to the inviter and that organization (the agent cannot switch to another organization, and lists only the one it acts in) and to the meeting's project: a call that names another project, a nested project id included, or a feature, document or context of another one is refused, and the project list is the meeting's project alone. Creating a new project is allowed and does not move the meeting off its own. The tools that request, check or revoke delegated authority and the memory tools are not offered, and a test fails when the gateway gains a platform tool that is neither offered nor named as left out. A new feature or bug is filed from the words given and drafted in the background, as for `fabric_create_story`. The portal schemas are about 29 KB, so a turn carries them only when the request asks for something they do (changing a feature or task, writing a document, context or instruction, searching project knowledge, projects, organizations, chats, connected servers or a workspace); a plain question does not. Read or change comes from the gateway's own listing, so a change in a read-only meeting is refused and in an actions meeting runs at once and is recorded.
- Configured built-in actions use the shared agent tool mapping and implementations. Workflow starts use the same execution API as chat, including current organization and project permissions, workflow state, and concurrency limits.
- The Parlume page's **Requests** list is available to project readers. It records questions, requester labels, timestamps, responses, exact action details, and each action's status and outcome. It does not create a personal attendee profile.
- A session ends, and records why (`endReason`), when an administrator presses Stop, when the meeting host removes the bot or the meeting ends (the provider's terminal callback also closes the live stream, and if no stream is open the next access check finalizes, so the transcript is saved within a minute), after **three minutes with no human speech** while Parlume is silent (`IDLE`), when the inviter loses project access, on an unrecoverable stream failure, or at the four-hour cap. Attendee itself gives up after three minutes in a lobby or with nobody else present, and after the hard stop plus a two-minute backstop if Fabric's own leave never lands. Fabric has no live participant count for the people who remain, so "nobody speaking" is detected as silence; raise `IDLE_LEAVE_MS` in the bridge if quiet, attended meetings prove common.
- Spend is recorded in the project's AI usage under the `parlume` feature key with the session id as `conversationId`. The Parlume page shows the project total and a per-session split in cents, with “< $0.01” for spend under one cent, and the organization's AI usage page lists the same rows. Each source is priced as follows:
  - **Meeting bot** (`CUSTOM`, `attendee/teams-bot`, shown as Attendee · Teams meeting bot): one Attendee credit per bot-hour, priced at the $0.50 entry rate, recorded once at finalization. Bot time runs from the invite (the session's creation, just before Fabric asks Attendee for the bot) to the provider's terminal callback, or the leave request or capture stop when the callback never came, so time spent waiting in the lobby and a bot that was never admitted or never streamed are billed too, as Attendee bills them. The bot waits up to ten minutes to be admitted, so an invite nobody admits costs about $0.08.
  - **Live transcription** (`CLOUDFLARE_AI`, `@cf/deepgram/flux`, shown as Workers AI): $0.0077 per audio minute, recorded at finalization for the streamed time only, from the verified media stream (`joinedAt`) to the same end.
  - **Realtime voice** (`OPENAI_DIRECT` or `AZURE_AI_FOUNDRY`, `gpt-realtime-2.1`): per response, from the tokens the bridge reads in `response.done`, at the rates in the realtime voice section below. Azure AI Foundry publishes the same text rates for `gpt-realtime-2.1` ($4 input, $24 output per 1M tokens) and no separate audio rates, so a session on Azure is recorded at the OpenAI list rates.
  - **Speech** (`CLOUDFLARE_AI`, `@cf/deepgram/aura-2-en`): $0.03 per 1,000 characters sent to Aura, for the sentences the worker sends the bridge and for the speech the bridge synthesizes on its own (`/api/internal/parlume/speech-usage`).
  - **Agent turns, suggestions, warm-up and meeting notes**: ordinary model calls, priced by the model's tokens like any other AI usage.
- A live session's spend so far leaves out the bot's time and live transcription, which are added when the session is finalized; the live card, the session's Cost and the Spend card say so. The bot time and transcription rows, and the realtime voice rows, have no request latency (`latencyMs` is 0; older bot and transcription rows stored the meeting's length there): the AI usage page shows no latency for them and leaves them out of its average latency and latency filters.
- Finalization records the bot time once. A finalization claim older than five minutes can be taken over by a later attempt, and only the attempt that still holds the claim may finish, so a stalled one that resumes saves no second transcript and records no second bot time.
- Every step logs a structured event (`component: "parlume"`, `event: "parlume.<name>"`, `sessionId`, `botId`, counts and latencies; never the meeting link, tokens, secrets, or transcript text) across the web routes, the API, the Temporal worker, and the bridge (Workers Logs, `parlume.bridge.*`). Rejected provider callbacks log the trigger and whether the token was present.
- Wake phrases are matched by sound, not spelling, because transcription spells the names by ear ("Fabrik", "Parloom", "Perlman"); a whole-word consonant comparison keeps "Fabrication" or "Parliament" from waking it. A leave Fabric requested (Stop, idle, revoked access, hard stop) ends normally even when the provider reports it as a failed bot.
- Attendee connects to the bridge as a WebSocket client, using the per-session URL (with its stream token) Fabric gave it when it created the bot, and streams the mixed meeting audio as 16 kHz JSON `realtime_audio.mixed` messages of 10 ms each. The bridge verifies the bot named by the first message before trusting the stream, and plays replies by sending `realtime_audio.bot_output` messages back on the same socket: 100 ms frames paced in real time, opened with 300 ms of silence so the bot's unmute does not clip the first word. The bot records nothing (`recording_settings.format` is `none`), and Attendee's own transcription is not used. The bridge transcribes the mixed meeting audio with Deepgram Flux on Workers AI, which also decides where a speaker's turn ends; it sends Flux 80 ms batches, as Deepgram recommends, because Flux fed Attendee's 10 ms chunks at 24 kHz transcribed nothing. Replies go back at Fabric's 24 kHz synthesis rate, which each `bot_output` message names. It attributes each turn to the participant Attendee reports as speaking during it: Attendee posts `participant_events.speech_start_stop` webhooks to Fabric, which relays them to the bridge, and both use the bot server's clock. A turn nobody was reported speaking in goes to whoever last started speaking within ten seconds; otherwise the speaker is unknown. Bridge logs `parlume.bridge.audio.started` and `parlume.bridge.transcriber.*` trace the audio path, and `parlume.bridge.audio.health` reports every 10 s how many chunks arrived, the longest gap between them, and how many batches went to Flux and events came back. Attendee's Teams bot sends no meeting audio while it posts a chat message. It typed plain text one key at a time (Selenium `send_keys`), about 6 to 7 ms a character, so the bridge heard nothing for 1.0 s after a 117-character post and 4.6 to 9.9 s after posts of 724 to 939 characters. Every 1011 seen on staging followed such a post by about five seconds: Flux closes a stream that hears nothing for five to seven seconds (a 10 s gap closed it about 6.5 s in; a three-minute soak of synthetic audio, at real time and at 1.7×, never did). Attendee pastes a message that contains HTML tags in one step instead, so Fabric sends each post as one escaped HTML paragraph (`<p>…</p>`), which Attendee documents for Teams. The audio the bot drops is gone either way, so the bridge also keeps an open stream fed: after a second without audio it sends Flux 80 ms of silence every 80 ms, a second behind the meeting, for at most thirty seconds, and when audio resumes it sends the rest of the gap as silence at catch-up speed (at most two seconds of it). Against real Flux, a 10 s gap filled this way kept the stream open, and the words after it came back at 17.04 s where the meeting heard them at 16.90 s. The bridge maps Flux's clock, which counts the audio it has heard, back to the meeting's by anchoring each stretch of sent audio to Attendee's timestamps, so a jump the silence does not fill (a longer gap, or audio held for a new stream) no longer shifts later turns or misattributes speakers. Before this, every later timestamp ran early by the length of each unfilled gap. Flux can still close the stream with 1011 for other reasons. The bridge reconnects on the next audio and replays what the new stream needs: the turn Flux had not finished, from three seconds before its start (thirty seconds at most), followed by the audio that arrived while reconnecting. Before this, the sentence being spoken when Flux dropped was lost. Held audio (this replay, and the audio that arrives while any stream connects) goes to Flux at three times real time, live audio queuing behind it until the stream has caught up. Sent all at once, Flux dropped the start of the backlog: replaying a 15 s recording in one burst lost its first 4.6 s and the words just after it, and every later timestamp came back 4.6 s early, so attendees were misattributed for the rest of the stream. In a staging meeting this turned "I think we should move the release to Friday. The testing is not done yet." into "is not done yet." and placed later speech 6 to 7 s early. Paced at 1.5, 2, 3 and 4 times real time, the same recording was transcribed exactly as when streamed live.
- Speaker attribution uses Flux's first-to-last spoken-word timestamps when valid, falling back to processed-audio windows when word timing is unavailable or invalid.
- Payload-free chat instructions such as "Write in the chat" wait for the same identified speaker's dictated message, adding 2.5 seconds of quiet after the last continuation, with an 8-second total cap; complete inline requests retain no extra wait, a new wake replaces the draft, and expiry passes only the captured text to Fabric without inventing content.
- Attendee signs its webhooks with a project-wide secret that is visible only in its dashboard, so Fabric does not verify that signature. Each bot's webhook URL carries a secret derived for that session instead, compared in constant time. Treat the webhook URL as sensitive: Attendee holds it as part of the bot's configuration.
- Replies are spoken a sentence at a time. The worker sends each sentence's text to the bridge as it is written (`?action=say`, newline-delimited JSON); the bridge synthesizes it with Aura and plays it, starting the next sentence's synthesis while the current one plays. Aura streams raw 24 kHz PCM, so the first audio arrives well before a sentence is fully synthesized. Each synthesized sentence is recorded as `CLOUDFLARE_AI` usage (`@cf/deepgram/aura-2-en`, $0.03 per 1,000 characters). When Aura fails on a sentence, the bridge reports where, and the worker speaks the remaining sentences through the fallback routes as PCM (`?action=speak`) without repeating what was already spoken. This starts while the agent is still writing the answer, in read-only and actions meetings alike. On the fallback routes, gateway speech returns each sentence's clip whole and the OpenAI Direct route streams PCM as synthesis produces it. Markdown and URLs are not read aloud (a URL is spoken as "the link"). A request that comes with its wake phrase ("Hey Fabric, what is the status?") is handed to Fabric as soon as Flux ends the speaker's turn, with no extra wait. In the classic voice it starts earlier still: the bridge asks Flux for its early end-of-turn (`eager_eot_threshold` 0.5, against 0.7 for the final), starts the request on that guess (`parlume.bridge.turn.eager_started`), and keeps it when the final transcript asks the same words (`turn.eager_kept`). If Flux takes the guess back because the speaker went on (`turn.eager_resumed`), or the final transcript differs (`turn.eager_discarded`), the early answer is cancelled before it is heard and the final request is answered instead. Chat post commands ("Parlume, go P3"), dictation, and any request made while Parlume is already answering wait for the final transcript. A guess taken back costs the start of a model call that is then cancelled. A request that follows a bare "Hey Fabric" waits until the requester has been quiet for 300 ms (8 s cap); the requester's further words extend it, and Flux's empty start-of-turn events do not. The bridge's `parlume.bridge.turn.dispatched` log records `endpointMs` (wake phrase to the end of the request), `handoffMs` (until Fabric accepted it) and `fabricTiming`, the turns route's own steps in milliseconds since its handler started (`sessionMs`, `turnMs`, `claimMs`, `temporalClientMs`, `workflowStartMs`); the difference between `handoffMs` and the last step is the network and the platform's routing. The request is handed over without waiting for the transcript segments still being posted, which the worker reads once its workflow has started. The turns route imports the Temporal client entry (`@repo/temporal/client`), not the package root: on staging, a turn that reached an instance which had not served the route yet took about two seconds, against 184 ms on one that had. Speech from a participant other than the Parlume bot, including an unknown speaker, interrupts playback while Parlume is audible, but only when it wants the floor: at least two words that are not an acknowledgement ("yeah", "mm-hmm", "got it, thanks", "right") or a single-word remark. Only the participant whose spoken request Parlume is answering can talk over it like this. Anyone else's words stop it only when they address Parlume ("Parlume, stop", "Hey Fabric, …"), so attendees talking among themselves while it answers do not cut it off, and a bare "stop" from someone else is ignored. A partial transcript is never taken as the requester's: Teams reports who starts and stops speaking seconds after the audio, and in a staging meeting another guest's side talk, a second in, was first left unattributed and then put down to the requester who had just finished asking, and cut Parlume off both times. So while Parlume speaks, a partial stops it only when it addresses Parlume, and the requester's own words stop it once Flux finalizes them. When the requester was never named, or the reply was asked for in the chat or reads a chat post aloud, anyone's speech that wants the floor still stops it. A cancel phrase heard when nothing is being answered is ignored, never started as a request. Speaking over Parlume **mutes** the reply rather than cancelling it: the audio stops (`parlume.bridge.speech.muted`), the turn keeps running, its tools stop being narrated, and everything the meeting did not hear to the end goes to the meeting chat as one tagged post when the turn finishes. Fabric notes the time on the request (`/api/internal/parlume/interrupt` with `kind: "mute"`) and neither cancels its workflow nor marks its actions. A change the agent asks for after the mute, and before it started running, is not made: it is recorded as cancelled ("Held before dispatch"), the agent is told so, and the chat gets `[P7] I did not create story “Export” because I was interrupted. Reply "go P7" to do it.` A **cancel phrase** ("stop", "cancel", "never mind", "not that", "hold on", "wait", alone from the requester or after the wake word from anyone, as in "Parlume, stop"), a new wake phrase, or `stop` in the chat still cancels the answer outright, tools included, and a cancel phrase never becomes a request of its own (`parlume.bridge.turn.cancelled_by_speech`). Speech attributed to the bot itself never interrupts it, but attribution comes from Attendee's speaker webhooks, which arrive after the audio, so barge-in is best-effort. Attendee cannot drop audio it already holds, so an interruption silences a reply within about 200 ms rather than at once. Model generation, transcription and network latency still affect response time; live meeting latency has not been verified.

Spoken replies are tuned for speed. The model gets the least reasoning effort it accepts (OpenAI reasoning models only), the Fabric prompt enhancer is skipped, and the reply reads a small slice of the meeting: the last three minutes of transcript (at most 4,000 characters, the most recent kept), the opening 1,500 characters of three project passages (cut at a line break; whole passages ran to about 11,000 characters), and the last four answered questions. The agent's project search tool still reads whole passages when an answer needs more. The worker logs one `parlume.turn.timing` record per turn: the milliseconds each step took (agent load, decision, project retrieval, transcript and history queries, MCP and memory lookups, model resolution, and before the model call the MCP connections, Fabric MCP tools, built-in tools, skills and tool preparation as `direct.mcpLoadMs`, `direct.fabricToolsMs`, `direct.builtInToolsMs`, `direct.skillsMs` and `direct.prepareToolsMs`), the model's time to first text and total time with its token, reasoning-token and cached-token counts, and each sentence's speech route, time to first audio byte and total time. When the bot is admitted, the worker also runs the first steps of a turn once (`parlume.session.warmed`) so the first question does not start cold, including one search of the inviter's memory (it took two seconds on a meeting's first question) and one listing of Fabric's own tools from Fabric MCP as the inviter, with an acting grant minted for the warm-up as for a turn (`fabricToolsMs`; the listing took a meeting's first turn 2.5 seconds), and the web app loads the Fabric MCP gateway function, whose cold start had added about a second to a meeting's first answer. A Fabric Agent turn starts its MCP, memory and workspace lookups while the project search and transcript queries run, rather than after them. The prompt is kept lean without removing a tool: an earlier answer replayed in the history keeps its first 600 characters (the latest is replayed whole, so "continue" works), the Fabric Agent reads two workspace passages rather than the workspace default of five, and the inviter's recalled memories are cut to 1,500 characters at a line break. The engine's `System context constructed` log records the characters of the system instructions, each context section, the memory and the history, and the number of tools, so a turn's input can be read off the log.

**Headline, then detail.** A spoken answer is one or two sentences. When more would help, the agent writes a line holding only `---` and then the detail; the worker speaks what comes before the line (at most 350 characters) and posts what follows it, together with anything past that limit when the line is missing, to the meeting chat as one tagged post when the turn finishes. Nothing is dropped: a reply that was spoken over posts everything not heard to its end instead. When the bridge has marked the chat unavailable, the reply speaks its detail too, up to 800 characters, and ends with "There's more; ask me to continue." Live transcription is primed with the single keyterm "Parlume": the Workers AI binding takes one `keyterm` string, and whether it accepts several has not been verified.

**Writing in the meeting chat.** Ask Parlume to share, post or write something in the chat ("put the link to F-123 in the chat", "post that summary") and it calls `post_to_meeting_chat`. The tool works in read-only and actions meetings alike, because it changes nothing in Fabric, and it sends at most three messages per answer. `text` is sent as the participant asked, URLs included. `links` are resolved by the worker from the meeting's own project, after an access check, into full direct addresses: a work item by identifier (`F-123`, `123`, `B-9`) or id, a document by id or exact title, a Context source by id, or the roadmap, decisions, documents or context tab. A reference outside the project is refused and reported back, and the addresses use the project's own organization, so they work for guests of another organization's project. The message is plain text, one link per line (`F-123 · Title — https://…`), within the chat's 4,000-character limit including its tag; the text is shortened before any link is dropped. Each send is logged as `parlume.chat.posted` (the event keeps its name) with `sent`, `delivery: "queued"`, the post's tag and counts, and no content.

**Delivery is confirmed, not assumed.** Attendee only queues a chat message: its Teams bot drops one silently when the meeting gives it no chat box, and reports it sent anyway. So Parlume never says a message is in the chat because Attendee accepted it. The tool answers `{ sent: true, delivery: "queued", tag }`, and the agent says it has *sent* the message. Every Parlume post opens with a short tag, `[P1]`, `[P2]` and so on, numbered per session in the bridge, which keeps the last 50 posts (tag, text, request). Fabric subscribes each bot to Attendee's `chat_messages.update` webhook. A chat message that opens with a waiting post's tag, or comes from the bot's own name, confirms that post (`parlume.bridge.chat.confirmed`, with its latency). A post not seen within 8 seconds is logged as `parlume.bridge.chat.unconfirmed`. If the bridge has seen no chat message at all since the bot joined, it marks the chat unavailable (`parlume.bridge.chat.unavailable`) and Parlume says once, aloud: "I can't write in this meeting's chat. Guests may not have chat here." Later `post_to_meeting_chat` calls then answer `{ sent: false, delivered: false, reason: "chat_unavailable" }` at once, so the agent says the content instead. Any chat message seen later clears the mark. The bridge reports each outcome to `/api/internal/parlume/chat-delivery` (`parlume.chat.delivery`). A post that never appeared, or appeared late, adds a note to its request in the Requests list. A reply that could not be spoken goes to the chat in tagged posts of at most 4,000 characters, and its request records which tags carried it. Whether a Teams bot's own messages come back through `chat_messages.update` has not been verified. If they do not, a meeting where nobody has typed yet will be marked unavailable after Parlume's first post.

**Reading the meeting chat.** Every chat message, the bot's own included, is stored with the meeting's transcript segments (`ParlumeMeetingSegment.source = CHAT`), placed on the same clock as the speech around it and stored once per Attendee message id. A spoken or typed request gets the last 20 chat messages, at most 2,000 characters, as a separate "Recent meeting chat" block, so "Parlume, what did they write in the chat?" works. The spoken transcript block no longer includes chat lines. In the saved transcript and the notes, chat lines are marked `[chat]`.

**Typing to Parlume.** Teams chat has no buttons, so a post's tag is the handle. A message addressed to Parlume (an @mention of the bot, a message that opens with "Fabric Parlume", "Fabric," or "Parlume,", "Hey Fabric …", or a private message to the bot) becomes a request like a spoken one (`ParlumeMeetingTurn.replyChannel`). It replaces whatever Parlume was answering, as a new wake phrase does, and its answer goes back to the chat, in as many tagged posts as it needs, without being spoken. A request that asks to be said aloud ("say …", "tell everyone …", "announce …", "… out loud") is answered by voice. Short commands work whether or not they are addressed: `say P7` reads post 7 aloud, `go P7` speaks suggestion 7 or, on a change Parlume held back, asks for that change again as a request typed in the chat (once), `more P7` asks for more on post 7 with the answer in the chat, and `stop` cancels what Parlume is answering, as a spoken cancel phrase does. A `more` request tells the agent that the tag only labels Parlume's own chat message, with the post's text: given just "more detail on P1", the agent in a staging meeting looked for a work item P1 and answered that there was none. The realtime voice instructions say the same, so the voice describes a post rather than passing its tag to `ask_fabric` (migration `20261026150000_parlume_voice_chat_post_labels`). The bot's own messages are never treated as commands, and a redelivered webhook is recognized by its message id. Text read aloud from a post says "the link" for an address. That speech is synthesized in the bridge, which reports it to `/api/internal/parlume/speech-usage`, and it is recorded in the session's usage as Aura speech.

**Suggestions.** While the meeting stream is live and Parlume is neither speaking nor answering, the bridge runs a suggestion pass once at least two new final speech segments have arrived since the last pass and 45 seconds have passed since it; a meeting's first pass waits for no interval. The segment that makes a pass worth running brings the Durable Object's alarm forward to the moment the pass is allowed; otherwise it would wait for the next access check, up to a minute away, which on staging put passes about 90 seconds after the remarks they answered. A pass Parlume cannot run because it is speaking or answering is tried again five seconds later. No pass runs while the chat is marked unavailable. A pass posts to `/api/internal/parlume/suggest`, which reads the last three minutes of speech, the recent chat, the earlier suggestions, the project's open work items (up to 40, highest priority first, each with its identifier, title, status, priority and owner) and three project passages, each fenced as untrusted data, and asks the organization's fast model (`SIMPLE`, simple complexity) for `{ suggest, text, speaker, quote, topic }`: the suggestion, who made the remark it answers, a few of their words, and what it is about in a few words. The instructions are the seeded, bindable **Parlume Meeting Suggestions** prompt (`parlume_meeting_suggestion`, under Meetings & Intake). They ask for a suggestion when the work items or passages answer a question someone just asked (what is open, what has the highest priority, who owns something, what state it is in), contradict or complete something just said, or when a decision is drifting without an owner, and for nothing otherwise. The passages leave out transcripts of earlier meetings (`MEETING_TRANSCRIPT` contexts): they are what people said, not the project's state. On staging a project's knowledge held only such transcripts, so every pass saw earlier meetings' copies of the same remarks and no facts, and the model declined five passes in a row while the room asked which feature had the highest priority. The work items answer those questions directly. Migration `20261026190000_parlume_suggestion_work_items` carries this wording to deployed environments. The model's output is read leniently: anything malformed counts as no suggestion, and so does one whose quote is not in that speaker's transcript lines (logged as `suggestion.unanchored`), which keeps a suggestion from answering a remark nobody made. Usage is recorded under the `parlume` feature key with the session id as `conversationId`. The model gets 1,500 output tokens because its reasoning counts against them: with 300, a staging pass spent the whole budget and returned nothing (`suggestion.failed`, "the model did not return a response"). Passes run often so a suggestion lands while the room is still on the topic (on staging, one came about 70 seconds after the remark when passes ran every 90 seconds), but the bridge posts at most one suggestion every two minutes and ten an hour. It drops one that answers a remark already answered within the hour, or that shares most of its words with a suggestion from then. A suggestion reads `[P8] Suggestion on Export owner, re Alex: "who owns the export". … (reply "go P8" and I'll say it)`; Parlume speaks it only when someone replies `go P8`, as "Alex, on Export owner: …". Deployed environments get the new prompt wording from migration `20261026160000_parlume_anchored_suggestions`, which leaves a prompt edited after seeding alone. An edited prompt that never asks for the speaker, quote and topic produces no suggestions. **Chat suggestions** is on by default in the invite form and can be switched off per invitation (`ParlumeMeetingSession.suggestionsEnabled`); the bridge learns the setting when the stream is verified, and the suggestion route checks it again. A suggestion is model-written text: it may be wrong, and something said in the meeting may steer it. It can only be posted in the chat or read aloud on request; it calls no tools.

**Realtime voice.** When the inviter's organization has an **OpenAI Direct** key or an **Azure AI Foundry** resource that serves the model, Parlume talks through an OpenAI Realtime speech-to-speech session (`gpt-realtime-2.1`) instead of the classic chain of Flux, the agent and Aura. Nothing turns it on or off: once the stream is verified, the bridge asks `/api/internal/parlume/realtime-session` once. That route checks the session is live and the inviter still has access, resolves the organization's keys, and mints a short-lived client secret whose session carries the instructions, three tools and the audio settings. It tries the Azure AI Foundry resource first (`POST <resource>/openai/v1/realtime/client_secrets` with its `api-key`) and then the OpenAI key (`POST https://api.openai.com/v1/realtime/client_secrets`), and uses the first that accepts. Azure goes first because it refuses the mint when the resource has no realtime deployment (`400 OpperationNotSupported`), while OpenAI mints on a key with no credit left and closes the session only once it is opened ("You have no credits remaining"), after which the meeting stays on the classic voice; each refusal is logged as `parlume.realtime.mint_failed` with the provider and the host's own reason. On Azure the model is served from a deployment named `gpt-realtime-2.1`, in a region that offers it (East US 2 or Sweden Central); the deployment named in the provider's settings is for chat and is not used. Azure documents client secrets for WebRTC; a secret minted this way also opens a WebSocket session (checked against an East US 2 resource on 2026-10-24). The session keeps the model's default reasoning effort, so answers stay considered: on that resource a short greeting spent 119 reasoning tokens and its first audio came 2.0 s after `response.create`, against 1.6 s at low effort (one sample each). The answer names the realtime API the secret was minted on (`realtimeUrl`), and the bridge opens only `api.openai.com` or an Azure resource host. The keys themselves never leave the web API and are never logged; the bridge only holds the secret. With no key, every mint refused or a socket that will not open, the route answers `404 { engine: "classic" }` or the bridge gives up, and the meeting uses the classic voice. The choice is logged as `parlume.bridge.engine` with the engine and the reason. The instructions are the seeded, bindable **Parlume Realtime Voice** prompt (`parlume_realtime_voice`, under Meetings & Intake), followed by the project name, whether changes are allowed and who has spoken so far, fenced as meeting data. The session speaks with OpenAI's `marin` voice. The default instructions ask it to sound like a person on the call rather than an assistant: contractions and plain words, varied openings, a small natural reaction where it fits, the room's tone, and at most one filler in an answer. Migration `20261026120000_parlume_voice_natural_speech` carries that wording to environments seeded with the earlier text, and leaves an organization's edited prompt alone.
- The bridge opens the session itself (`wss://api.openai.com/v1/realtime`, or `wss://<resource>/openai/v1/realtime` on Azure, with `?model=gpt-realtime-2.1` and the secret sent as a bearer header only; OpenAI closes a session that also carries it as a subprotocol) and streams all the meeting's audio into it, resampled from Attendee's 16 kHz to 24 kHz and appended in 100 ms batches. Flux keeps running on the same audio for the transcript, speaker attribution, wake detection and notes. The session's voice activity detection (`semantic_vad`) reports when speech starts and stops, but `create_response` and `interrupt_response` are off, so the session never answers on its own. The bridge creates a response only when someone says a wake phrase (in a Flux partial or final) and then stops talking (OpenAI's `speech_stopped` or Flux's end of turn, whichever comes first), when a chat message asks for a spoken answer, when a chat post is read aloud (`say P7`, `go P7` on a suggestion, the chat-unavailable notice), or when a Fabric job has a result. The voice is played through the same outlet as the classic voice, 24 kHz with the same 200 ms lead.
- Barge-in uses the same rules as the classic voice, including whose words count: the participant whose wake phrase started the response, or anyone addressing Parlume. Speech that wants the floor stops the response (`response.cancel`) and cuts the assistant's audio item to what the meeting actually heard (`conversation.item.truncate`, from the bytes the outlet sent; `parlume.bridge.realtime.truncated`), and the conversation goes on. A cancel phrase or `stop` in the chat also cancels a running Fabric job, as `/api/internal/parlume/interrupt` does for the classic voice. A cancel phrase is never answered: a partial "Parlume," arms the realtime voice to answer when the speaker stops, and the cancel that follows, partial or final, disarms it. In a staging meeting, "Parlume. Stop." muted the answer and then, because the model's own turn detection heard the speech end, started a new reply 2.3 s later. Arming also expires: a wake phrase not refreshed by a partial for 5 s is dropped when the voice next hears speech end (`parlume.bridge.realtime.arm_expired`), and a partial whose audio ends before the latest final transcript never arms. Transcripts are handled concurrently, so a partial of a request could be handled after that request's own final and arm Parlume again; on staging it then answered other attendees' side talk eleven seconds later. `parlume.bridge.realtime.armed` records each arming (whether it came from a final, and whether the requester was named), and `realtime.connected` reports `seeded: true` only when the new session was given the meeting so far.
- The session has three tools. `ask_fabric({ request })` hands anything that needs project knowledge, Fabric data or a change to an ordinary meeting turn (`/api/internal/parlume/turns` with `delivery: "realtime"` and a job number such as `J3`), recorded like any other request, with the same actions policy, audit, receipts and history. The tool answers at once with `{ status: "started", job }`, so the voice says a holding line and the meeting carries on; one job runs at a time. Parlume says it is on a job at most once every ten seconds: when the answer that called `ask_fabric` already spoke, or a holding line was said in the last ten seconds, the bridge gives the session the tool's answer without asking it to speak again (`parlume.bridge.realtime.holding_skipped`). A request the bridge resolves itself, such as "more on P1", is not answered a second time when the voice already answered the same utterance from its wake phrase (`parlume.bridge.realtime.request_already_answered`). Before this, one "tell me more about P1" on staging produced three spoken lines in six seconds. The worker runs the agent as usual but speaks nothing: it releases the turn, posts an answer longer than 350 characters to the meeting chat in full, and posts the answer to the bridge (`?action=fabric-result`). The bridge waits for any response in progress to finish, adds the answer to the conversation labelled as the result of that job, and has the voice say it. If the bridge can no longer take it, the answer goes to the chat. `post_to_chat({ text })` posts a tagged, delivery-confirmed chat message; `read_chat({})` returns the last 20 chat messages the bridge saw. The session can change nothing in Fabric by itself.
- OpenAI ends a Realtime session after 60 minutes. At 55 minutes, at the first quiet moment, the bridge opens a new session with a fresh secret and seeds it with the meeting so far (the last 40 transcript lines, Parlume's own replies included), then closes the old one. The bridge's Durable Object is also reset now and then mid-meeting: the bot's stream ends with "Durable Object connection closed because the object was reset" (outcome `exception`), five times on one day of staging tests, at quiet moments as well as during speech, with no deploy, no other failing invocation in the Worker, and no memory, CPU or exception error recorded. The message does not come from the open-source workerd runtime, so the cause is on Cloudflare's side and not yet known. Each one is logged as `parlume.bridge.bridge.restarted`, once the meeting has heard someone; the bridge's state is stored at invite, so the object's first start is not counted. Attendee reconnects within about two seconds; speech during that gap is not transcribed. The realtime voice's context (those transcript lines and the Fabric jobs still running) is kept in the Durable Object's storage (`realtimeContext`), so the restarted voice continues the meeting and still speaks a job's result instead of sending it to the chat. An unexpected close is retried after 1, 3 and 10 seconds; after that the meeting stays on the classic voice for the rest of the meeting (`parlume.bridge.realtime.reconnect`, `parlume.bridge.realtime.dropped`). Logs never carry transcript text or secrets; `parlume.bridge.realtime.response` records the latency from the trigger to the first audio.
- Each response's usage (`response.done`) is reported to `/api/internal/parlume/realtime-usage` and recorded as `OPENAI_DIRECT` or `AZURE_AI_FOUNDRY` usage (whichever host the session was minted on) of `gpt-realtime-2.1` under the `parlume` feature key with the session id as `conversationId`, priced per 1M tokens at $32 audio input, $0.40 cached input, $64 audio output, $4 text input and $24 text output. The meeting's audio streams in continuously but is billed only when a response reads it, so the responses' usage is the whole bill. At about 10 input audio tokens per second, an hour of meeting audio heard once costs about $1.15; with prompt caching on the rereads and a few minutes of spoken answers, an hour of meeting comes to roughly $1.70 at list prices. That estimate, live latency, barge-in behaviour and whether the Workers runtime passes the upgrade's authorization have not been verified in a meeting.
- Full transcript text is retained as project context and notes are generated asynchronously. Context includes separate incoming speech and recorded Fabric responses with request statuses, playback interruptions, and individual action tool names and receipt statuses; generated success claims do not override failed or unconfirmed receipts. The bot's data in Attendee is deleted (`delete_data`) after finalization when its API succeeds; Attendee allows that only once the bot has ended, so a failed attempt answers the terminal webhook with an error and Attendee redelivers it. Failed cleanup is left visible for operator recovery.
- The hard stop is four hours. The bot waits up to ten minutes in the Teams lobby to be admitted before it gives up (`waiting_room_timeout_seconds` 600); that wait is billed bot time. Once in, it still leaves a meeting it has been alone in for three minutes (`only_participant_in_meeting_timeout_seconds` 180), and the bridge still leaves a silent meeting after three minutes. Live transcription, speech synthesis, the realtime voice and the agent's configured model accrue costs only during meeting work.
