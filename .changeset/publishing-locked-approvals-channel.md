Point a publishing prompt's approval rules at the decisions a project member actually settled, so an approval claimed only in source material no longer clears them and only a decision that grants permission can.

Case study, newsletter blurb, stakeholder email and webinar script prompts now carry a locked list of the approval decisions a project member settled on the topic — customer names, assets, metrics, internal UI and video walkthroughs, and for case studies and webinar scripts how much of the implementation may be described. Their approval and disclosure rules, and a case study's report of whether the customer may be named and its metrics treated as confirmed, are satisfied only by a decision in that list that grants permission, and an asset such a decision names is listed as confirmed only if the decision approves it; an asset no decision names is still judged from the topic's context as before. A refusal is listed with its answer and grants nothing, as does an answer too long to show in full, and an approval claimed only in a document, the run's guidance or the topic no longer satisfies those rules. All seven drafting prompts also state that the editable prompt's "unless the context above explicitly marks them safe to share" exception is not met by source material, which reaches an organization's own edited prompt as well.
